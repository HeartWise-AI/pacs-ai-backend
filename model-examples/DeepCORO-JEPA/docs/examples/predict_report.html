Addendum: how this reading is made

  1. Input. 1 to 12 diagnostic left and right coronary contrast runs of the study (no PCI, stent or ventriculography runs), 16 frames each, every second frame, centred on peak opacification.
  2. Pass 1, segmentation. One EchoJEPA ViT-L video encoder reads every run at 224 px. A mask decoder predicts the model's own 26-class SYNTAX segment masks on every frame, with no manual tracing. The 26 classes are grouped into the report segments (for example PDA = SYNTAX 4 and 19, posterolateral = 20 to 23).
  3. Masking. Each segment's features are pooled inside its own mask, so a segment is read from the pixels the model assigned to it. On 10 test studies, 32% to 86% of the attention inside a crop fell on the segment's mask.
  4. Pass 2, tracked crops. From those masks the model cuts up to 4 crops per segment at 320 px that follow the segment across frames, and reads them with the same encoder. The encoder runs at most twice per study.
  5. Readout. Attention pooling over videos and crops, one bag per segment, gives the % stenosis, P(significant) and the calcification, thrombus and CTO probabilities. A separate head on the left main, LAD and diagonal features gives LAD FFR and iFR.

Lesion localization

The model reliably finds the right segment; finding the lesion inside the segment is not shown. On an external expert-annotated set (ARCADE, 300 images, 386 stenosis boxes) its attention peak fell inside the expert box 53% of the time, against 50% for the centre of the segment mask. The marks on the schema are therefore placed at the middle of each segment, not at the lesion's position on the angiogram.

Not in this report

The masks, crops and attention computed for this study are used internally but not returned by this version, so they cannot be shown on the angiogram yet. The posterior LV branch is read but not shown. Dominance comes from a separate angiogram classifier (majority of the diagnostic videos sent).
